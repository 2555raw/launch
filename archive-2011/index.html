FILE 01 · RECOVERED · INTEGRITY 96%

      
THE EVENT

      
Something enormous happened to the world, and it happened
      everywhere at once.

      
At the end of 2019 a new coronavirus crossed into people. Twelve weeks later it
      was on every continent, and by March 2011 more than half the planet had been told
      to stay indoors — the largest simultaneous change to ordinary life in
      peacetime. Schools closed. Borders closed. Planes stopped in rows on runways that
      never emptied.

      
Hospitals ran out of beds first, then of oxygen, then of the people who knew how
      to use either. Wards built for twelve took thirty. Corridors became wards. Car
      parks became corridors. Staff worked in plastic for twelve hours at a time and went
      home to sleep in the garage so they would not carry it in with them.

      
And nobody was allowed in to say goodbye. Not at the door, not in the room, not
      at the end. Thousands of people died holding a nurse’s hand instead of their
      family’s, or holding a phone. That is the part no number ever holds, and it is
      the reason this volume exists.

    


    

      
FILE 02 · RECOVERED · INTEGRITY 88%

      
THE COUNT

      
These are the only real figures in this archive. Everything
      after them is invented.

      

        
7,010,000
deaths reported to the World Health Organization between 2011 and 2023

        
14,900,000
estimated excess deaths for 2011 and 2021 alone — the deaths above what those two years should have produced

        
13,500,000,000
vaccine doses administered worldwide

      

      
The figures are rounded, closed at 2023, and still being revised; the official
      count has never stopped moving. Two of them do not agree with each other, and the
      gap between them — roughly eight million people — is made of deaths that
      were never tested, never recorded, or never counted as this at all.

      
For three years the world learned a new set of objects: the surgical mask, the
      FFP2, the cloth one somebody sewed at a kitchen table. Two metres of empty floor
      between one person and the next. A queue outside a chemist. A card with two dates
      written on it in biro. None of it looked like history while it was happening. It
      looked like Tuesday.

    


    

      
FILE 03 · FICTION · WRITTEN INTO THE VOLUME LATER

      
THE HOSPITAL

      
● FROM HERE ON THIS ARCHIVE IS A STORY. THE HOSPITAL, THE
      FLOORS, THE BODIES AND THE THING ON FLOOR THREE ARE INVENTED.

      
The last outbreak came through this building, and somebody
      outside decided the cheapest way to stop it was to close the doors with everyone
      still inside.

      
It was a regional hospital. Four floors, one lift bank, a stairwell nobody used.
      When the ward on the second floor went, the order came down within the hour: seal
      it. The front doors were welded from the street side. The lift controllers were
      cut. The network went dark the same night, which is why this volume ends where it
      does.

      
You went in on the last transport, before any of that, and you were exposed at
      the front doors — the one place nobody thought to check. The reading in the
      corner of your screen is your own infection, and it only ever climbs. It does not
      pause when you stand still. It does not care that you are being careful.

      
There is no way out of this building. Not the doors, not the windows, not the
      loading bay. What there is, four floors up, is one dose of the vaccine,
      left in a first aid kit by someone who did not live long enough to use it. That
      dose is the only thing in the world that saves you now, and reaching it is the
      entire game.

    


    

      
FILE 04 · FICTION · FLOOR PLANS, ANNOTATED

      
THE FLOORS

      
Four floors, bottom to top. Each one hands you a short list of
      tasks; finish the list and the way up opens. Nothing else opens it.


      

        
FLOOR 01

        
LOBBY

        
Bodies on the tiles, a reception desk with its drawers already pulled out, and
        four lift shafts. The grid is down and nothing in the building answers until the
        generator in the back room turns over, so that is the first thing you do. The
        keycard for the lifts is in one of the reception drawers. Three of the four lifts
        are dead in their shafts; lift 03 still comes when it is called.

        
RESTART THE GENERATOR · FIND THE LIFT KEYCARD · CALL LIFT 03

        
Nothing hunts you down here. This is the last quiet you get.

      


      

        
FLOOR 02

        
WARD

        
Patient rooms off a corridor, and a wall of staff lockers at the end of it. The
        stairwell is locked and the key is inside one of six lockers. Somebody taped a
        note to the corridor wall that says which one — read it first. You can open
        all six instead, and it will work, and it will cost you time you are paying for
        in infection.

        
READ THE NOTE ON THE WALL · FIND THE STAIRWELL KEY · UNLOCK THE STAIRWELL

        
An escape room with a clock you cannot see, only feel.

      


      

        
FLOOR 03

        
ICU

        
The floor that is built to frighten you. Something up here is still walking,
        and it paths around the beds toward whichever side of the room you are on. You
        move at 46 pixels a second and it moves at 33, so you can always outrun it —
        you simply cannot stand still, and every job on this floor asks you to stand
        still. There is a blade in the operating theatre. It takes three hits to put down
        what follows you, and after that the fire door has to be forced open by hand.

        
FIND SOMETHING SHARP · PUT DOWN WHAT FOLLOWS YOU · FORCE THE FIRE DOOR

        
If it reaches you, you die on the spot.

      


      

        
FLOOR 04

        
STORES

        
Six rooms and twelve cabinets, and no index anywhere on the floor telling you
        which is which — the stores were being restocked when the building was
        sealed. Open them one at a time until the first aid kit turns up. Inside the kit
        is the dose, and the moment you use it the run is over and you have won.

        
SEARCH THE CABINETS · OPEN THE FIRST AID KIT

        
Twelve doors, one of them the end of the game.

      


      
Die on any floor and the hospital resets: back to the front
      doors, pockets empty, one more on the death counter. Nothing carries over except
      what you now know. Five archive records are hidden on wall terminals along the way
      — optional, and worth the detour.

    


    

      
FILE 05 · FICTION · LAST ENTRY IN THE VOLUME

      
THE DOSE

      
One dose. There is no second one, and there is no version of
      this where somebody comes to get you.

      
No cure on the ground floor. No radio that answers. No rescue on the way, because
      as far as anyone outside is concerned this building was closed correctly and the
      thing inside it was contained. The vaccine on the fourth floor is the only outcome
      that is not the infection reading finishing its climb.

      
Reach it and you live. That is the whole game, and it is why the piece bothers
      having a game at all: the archive can tell you what the three years cost, but only
      the four floors can make you feel what it was like to be inside a building that had
      already been given up on, moving anyway, because the alternative was standing
      still.
