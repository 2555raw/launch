How it works

Pick a frog. Name your coin. Bond them.

Every stock on the pond is a frog. Your coin rides on one of them: it is priced in that stock, trades against that stock, and the pool between the two is locked from the first block. Three steps, one signature.

Step 1

Pick the stock

Choose the frog your coin will ride: NVIDIA, Tesla, Apple, the S&P 500 and the rest. From that moment your coin is quoted in that share, not in ETH.

Step 2

Name your coin

A name, a ticker, a picture and a line about it. One billion supply, fixed. No mint, no owner keys. Buy first if you want to be the first holder.

Step 3

Bond and trade

One signature deploys your coin, opens the pool against the stock and locks the liquidity for good. Your pair is live on the board the same second, ready to trade.

Why bond to a stock

A price you can reason about, a story that writes itself.

ETH pairs make every new coin a leveraged bet on ETH. Bonding to a stock swaps that beta for something people already follow.

The narrative is built in

A coin bonded to TSLA is a Tesla coin the moment it exists. Earnings, launches, headlines: the stock brings the attention, your token rides it.

Quoted in a unit people know

Prices read as fractions of a share, not of a gas token. Holders can tell in a glance whether the coin moved or the whole market did.

Liquidity that cannot leave

The pool is locked at deploy and the contract has no owner. Nobody, including you, can pull the stock out from under holders.

FAQ

Questions people ask before the first bond.

What does "bonded to a stock" actually mean?

Your token's only liquidity pool has a tokenized stock on the other side instead of ETH or a stablecoin. Buying your token means paying in that stock; the price is a number of shares per token. Nothing is pegged: the token trades freely, it is just quoted and settled in the share.

Do I need to own the stock to launch?

No. You pay the creation fee and, if you want, an optional first buy. Bonded seeds the pool from the token supply; the stock side fills as people buy in.

Can I remove the liquidity later?

No, and neither can we. The pool position is locked at deploy with no withdraw function. That is the whole point of a bond.

What do creators earn?

A share of the swap fee on every trade in the pair, for as long as it trades. The exact split is listed in the Verify section above and in the docs.

What happens when the stock market is closed?

Tokenized stocks trade on chain around the clock; their price follows the underlying and can drift while the market is shut. Your pair keeps trading, quoted in that token, at whatever the pool says.

Is this an offer of securities?

Bonded does not issue stocks. It builds pools between tokens you create and tokenized shares issued by third parties on —. Read the docs and your local rules before launching or trading.
