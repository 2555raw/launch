How it works

Pick a frog. Name your coin. Bond them.

Every stock on the pond is a frog. Your coin rides on one of them: it is priced in that stock, trades against that stock, and the pool between the two is locked from the first block. Three steps, one signature.

Step 1

Pick the stock

Choose the frog your coin will ride: NVIDIA, Tesla, Apple, the S&P 500 and the rest. From that moment your coin is quoted in that share, not in ETH.

Step 2

Name your coin

A name, a ticker, a picture and a line about it. One billion supply, fixed. No mint, no owner keys. Buy first if you want to be the first holder.

Step 3

Bond and trade

One signature deploys your coin, opens the pool against the stock and locks the liquidity for good. Your pair is live on the board the same second, ready to trade.

Why bond to a stock

A price you can reason about, a story that writes itself.

ETH pairs make every new coin a leveraged bet on ETH. Bonding to a stock swaps that beta for something people already follow.

The narrative is built in

A coin bonded to TSLA is a Tesla coin the moment it exists. Earnings, launches, headlines: the stock brings the attention, your token rides it.

Quoted in a unit people know

Prices read as fractions of a share, not of a gas token. Holders can tell in a glance whether the coin moved or the whole market did.

Liquidity that cannot leave

The pool is locked at deploy and the contract has no owner. Nobody, including you, can pull the stock out from under holders.

Before you trade

What a bonded coin is, and is not

Three things worth reading before any transaction. None of them are hidden in a footer, and none of them change once a pair is open.

01

It is not a share.

A coin bonded to NVDA is priced in NVDA and trades against it. It does not give you NVIDIA stock, dividends or votes. The stock is the other side of the pool, nothing more.

02

The stock is permanent.

A pair cannot be re-bonded after launch. Pick the stock your buyers actually follow, because that decision outlives every other one you make.

03

Locked liquidity is not value.

The pool cannot be pulled, by you or by us. That protects holders from a rug; it does not make a coin worth anything. Treat the price as a live reading, not a forecast.

A few things you might wonder

Questions

What is a pair here?

A coin, bonded to the stock it is priced in. You pick the stock at launch; the coin's price is quoted in that stock and every trade settles in it too, rather than being converted through ETH first.

Do I need to own the stock to launch?

No. You pay the creation fee and, if you want, an optional first buy. Bonded seeds the pool from the coin supply; the stock side fills as people buy in.

Can I remove the liquidity later?

No, and neither can we. The pool position is locked at launch with no withdraw function. That is the whole point of a bond.

What do creators earn?

A share of the swap fee on every trade in the pair, for as long as it trades. The exact split is listed in the Verify section and in the docs.

What happens when the stock market is closed?

Tokenized stocks trade on chain around the clock; their price follows the underlying and can drift while the market is shut. Your pair keeps trading, quoted in that token, at whatever the pool says.

Is any of this real?

The pairs on this site are sample data until the adapter is wired to the chain. Bonded does not issue stocks; it builds pools between coins you create and tokenized shares issued by third parties on —. Read the docs and your local rules before launching or trading.
