How it works

Hydro pairs a token with one specific water source. What follows is the whole model, exactly as the demo implements it.

The register

There are 32 sources in four classes: reservoirs, aquifers, glaciers and desalination. Each one carries a venue (the body that measures it), a quality assay, a unit — acre-foot, thousand cubic metres or tonne — and a spot price.

Availability and spot

Every source has a fill level between 0 and 1. The fill drifts over time and drags the spot with it: when available water falls, spot rises. Scarcity sets the price, not volume.

The pairing

At launch the token fixes spot0, its source's spot at that moment, and opens at a price derived from the fill. From there:

  • price = price0 · (spot / spot0)β · (1 + curve/4.2 · 0.65)
  • β is the sensitivity the creator picks, between 0.2× and 3×.
  • Curve is what has been raised against the graduation target.

Curve and graduation

Each token trades on a bonding curve up to 4.2 ETH. Until then the stage shows how far the curve has run; once it completes, the token graduates and the pool opens.

The vault

3% of every trade, buy or sell, goes into the token's vault. In the demo nothing is distributed or claimed: it accumulates and stays visible on the token page.

What this is not

A demo. There is no chain, wallet, custody or real water right behind any ticker. The bodies and reservoirs named here exist, but here they only label simulated data generated and kept in your own browser. "Reset data", in the footer, wipes all of it.
