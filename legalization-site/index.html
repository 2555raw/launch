RESEARCH PROJECT

LEGALIZATION?

Thirteen substances, one question: for each of them, does banning it prevent more harm than regulating it would?

13 SUBSTANCES 3 TIERS SIDE EFFECTS SHOWN @useLegalization

01 / THE QUESTION

Legal does not mean safe

Alcohol and tobacco are sold in every supermarket, and together they kill more people than every illegal drug combined. Meanwhile, substances that far fewer people use are treated as crimes. Today's legal line is historical, not scientific.

So the useful question is not "should drugs be legal?" but this: for each substance, which system causes the least harm, prohibition, decriminalization, supervised medical use, or strict regulation? The answer is different for cannabis than for fentanyl, and that is the whole argument.

02 / VOCABULARY

Four words people mix up

PROHIBITION

It is a crime. The market still exists, run entirely by criminals, with no control over dose or purity.

DECRIMINAL.

Using is no longer punished, but nothing legal is sold. Portugal has worked this way since 2001.

LEGALIZATION

It stops being illicit and can be sold. On its own it says nothing about how.

REGULATION

The rules: age limits, potency caps, labels, taxes, no advertising. This is what decides the outcome.

03 / THE MODEL

Three tiers, not one switch

Instead of "legalize everything" or "ban everything", each substance goes to the system that handles it best.

TIER 1

REGULATED SALE

Legal shops under strict rules: minimum age, potency limits, labels, taxes and no advertising.

TIER 2

MEDICAL ONLY

No shops. Use inside clinical protocols, supervised, with a product of known composition.

TIER 3

NO MARKET

Nothing is sold legally. The user stops being a criminal case and becomes a health case: treatment, not prison.

06 / CONCLUSION

The answer is not one word

THE QUESTION IS NOT WHETHER A DRUG SHOULD BE LEGAL OR ILLEGAL, BUT WHICH SYSTEM CAUSES THE LEAST HARM FOR THAT DRUG.

By that test: cannabis fits regulated sale, the psychedelics and ketamine fit supervised medical use, and for heroin, fentanyl, meth and cocaine the answer is not shops but treatment instead of prison. Alcohol and tobacco are the warning label on the whole project, because legal has never meant safe.
