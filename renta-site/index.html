The ones people
actually ask.

A token called vRENTA, which is a share of one vault. The vault owns ten apartment buildings through a Dutch holding company, and it owns them outright. You are not buying a specific flat and you are not lending anybody money — you hold a slice of everything the vault holds, including the cash it has not spent yet.

No. Nothing is ever sent to you. At each monthly close the rent that survived the bills stays inside the vault, so the vault is worth more and every vRENTA is worth more with it. You turn that into money by selling your shares back, whenever you like. It means one taxable event instead of twelve, and no dust payments.

A wallet, 100 EURG and about forty seconds. No credit check, no notary, no deposit saved over five years, and no minimum holding period. The deposit is a single transaction and the vault prices it at the share price standing at that moment.

You sell vRENTA back to the vault in one transaction and EURG arrives in the same block. The vault keeps a cash reserve — €430,593 at the last close — so ordinary exits settle instantly. A redemption larger than the reserve is queued until the next close, which is the one case where you wait.

Leaving early is priced. Anyone who redeems within ninety days of depositing sells back slightly below the share price, and the difference does not go to us — it stays in the vault and lifts the price for everyone still in. It is the second line in the calculator above. It exists so that a nervous month does not force the vault to sell a building to pay people who arrived three weeks ago.

No, and that is deliberate. One building is one roof, one boiler and one bad tenant away from a very bad year. Spread across seventy-seven apartments in eight cities, an empty flat costs the vault a few hundred euros a month rather than everything.

Because we only buy what the rent can pay for. There is no mortgage anywhere in this structure, so the vault grows at the speed of deposits and retained rent, not at the speed of a bank. Every purchase is announced in The Roll before it completes, with the price paid and the leases already in place.

The managing agent chases it, the close records it as rent scheduled but not collected, and the share price rises by a little less that month. You saw exactly that in August: one lease in Rotterdam ended on the ninth, collection fell by €70 against July, and the line kept going up anyway.

The buildings are owned by the holding company, not by us, and the holders of vRENTA own the holding company. If the front end goes dark the vault contract still accepts redemptions from any wallet, and the deeds do not care whether this website exists.
