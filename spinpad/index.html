FAQ

Questions

What is Twistr?

A coin launchpad where you do not choose the pairing. You make the coin; the wheel decides what it is paired with.

How does the wheel work?

It lands on a body position and a colour. Four positions by four colours is sixteen outcomes, one per asset, each drawn one time in sixteen.

Can I choose my pairing?

No. That is the product.

Can I reroll?

No. One spin, one pairing. You can throw the whole draft away and start again from an empty form, but that is starting over, not rerolling.

What happens after I spin?

The pairing locks, the confirmation screen shows exactly what will be deployed, and launching writes that pairing into the coin's contract where nothing can change it.

What assets can I pair with?

The sixteen on the board. They are names written into the contract — there is no oracle and nothing tracks a share price.

How do I launch a coin?

Connect a wallet, fill in four fields, spin, confirm. The deployment is a transaction from your own address.

Which chain is Twistr using?

Base. The pad checks it is on the right network before it will send anything.

Launching deploys a real contract and costs real money. Nothing here is an investment, an offer or advice, and no outcome is promised — the pairing is decided by a wheel. Asset names are written into the coin as a label and imply no relationship with, or endorsement by, those companies; nothing tracks a share price. Before you spin

It is a game. The money is not.

The wheel is the game. What happens when you press launch is a transaction on Base that nobody can take back. The short version:

  • Launching deploys a real ERC-20 on Base. It costs gas, it is permanent, and it comes from your address. No undo, no support desk, no refund.
  • You do not pick the pairing. A wheel does, once. That is not a bug we are fixing.
  • Opening a pool moves real funds. It is optional, separately confirmed, and a new pool with thin liquidity is trivially easy for anyone to drain.
  • Nothing here is an investment and nobody is advising you. A coin made in an afternoon is worth whatever someone will pay for it, which is usually nothing.
