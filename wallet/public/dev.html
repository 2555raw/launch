Give it the address the plan payments go to, and a passphrase. The address is encrypted with that passphrase before it is stored, so anyone who opens this page without it sees nothing at all: no address, no balances, no payments.

What the lock does, and what it cannot do. It keeps this page shut: the address is sealed in this browser with AES-GCM, under a key stretched from your passphrase by 310,000 rounds of PBKDF2. There is no way in without the passphrase and no way to reset it, because there is nowhere a reset could come from. What no lock can change is the chain itself: payments to an address are public, so anyone who already has the address can read the same figures on a block explorer. This hides your page, not the blockchain.
