How it works

Three steps and you're taking payments.

  1. 1

    You make the wallet

    The browser generates twelve words and shows them once. You write them down, and we ask for three at random to check you really have them.

  2. 2

    You set a password

    It encrypts the wallet on your device. Without it, what's stored is useless to anyone, including whoever steals your laptop.

  3. 3

    You get paid

    Make a link with the amount and what it's for. Whoever opens it sees the payment filled in and just confirms. The money arrives in seconds.

Why "Ward"

A lock doesn't keep things safe by trusting you.

Inside a warded lock sit the wards: fixed obstructions machined into the mechanism. They don't check who you are and they ask nobody's permission. Either the cuts in your key clear them, or the lock doesn't turn. That is the whole security model, and it has no customer service department.

This wallet works the same way. There is no account to suspend, no support line that can let someone in, and no server holding a copy of anything worth stealing. A payment moves because a key that only you have produced the right signature. Geometry, not trust.

The other meaning is the older one. To ward is to guard, and what is in your ward is what you have taken responsibility for. That is the honest description of what you are doing here: nobody is minding it for you.

Security

Why this isn't the same as leaving money on a website.

Most sites that "hold your crypto" are ordinary custody under a new name: your money sits on their server and you hold a promise. Here there is no server that can spend it, because there is no server that knows the key.

The key is born and dies in your browser

Generated with the system's cryptographic source, encrypted with scrypt and AES, and stored only on your device. It never travels over the network, not even encrypted.

No outside script can run here

The signing library is served from this domain rather than a CDN, and the content policy blocks every other script. That is how keys get stolen in most cases, and that door is bricked up.

If we disappear, your money doesn't

Your twelve words are an open standard (BIP-39). They work in MetaMask, Rainbow, Ledger or any other wallet. You do not depend on this page staying up.

You can read the code

It is a static page: open your browser's tools and read exactly what it does, line by line. Nothing runs on a server you cannot see.

Before you begin

Know exactly what you are holding.

Self-custody is a real trade. You gain a wallet nobody can freeze, and you take on the part a bank normally carries. These five points are the whole of it, stated plainly so there is nothing to discover later.

01

A wallet, not a card

Ward moves value between blockchain addresses. Paying at a shop terminal requires a licensed card issuer and BIN sponsorship, which is a banking contract rather than a piece of software. If you need to tap at a till, you need a card product, and this is not one.

02

You are the custodian

Ward is not a bank and holds no deposits, so no deposit protection scheme applies. What protects the balance is your own key and your own backup, which is exactly why both matter so much.

03

Your twelve words are the only spare key

Passwords here cannot be reset, because nobody holds a copy to reset them against. The recovery phrase restores the wallet on any device, in Ward or in any other BIP-39 wallet. Write it on paper and store that paper the way you store cash.

04

Settlement is final

A confirmed transaction cannot be recalled, reversed or charged back by anyone, Ward included. That is what makes the money arrive in seconds instead of days, and it is why every payment passes a review screen showing the recipient, the network and the fee before you sign.

05

Value moves with the market

ETH and POL are volatile assets, and a balance held in them can be worth more or less tomorrow. Getting paid in USDC or USDT keeps the amount you agreed on, which is what most people invoicing in Ward choose.

Questions

What people usually ask.

Can you get at my money?

No, and it is not a matter of trust but of architecture. The key that authorises a payment is generated in your browser and stays encrypted on your device. Our server only hands out files and never receives the key, encrypted or otherwise, so there is nothing for us to spend, freeze, or hand to anyone.

How does topping up from MetaMask work?

Your browser wallet announces itself to the page, you approve a connection, and then you approve one ordinary transfer to your Ward address. Ward never sees that wallet's private key. It can only ask it to sign, and you can always say no. Coinbase Wallet, Phantom, Rainbow and the rest work the same way.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Nothing, if you saved your twelve words. Install the wallet on another device, type them in, and the same balance is there. Whoever finds the phone meets an encrypted file and a password they do not know.

And if I lose the twelve words?

Then access to the money is gone for good. This is not a policy we can bend: no copy of those words exists anywhere except where you put them. Write them on paper and keep that paper the way you would keep cash.

What does the paid plan actually get me?

Software features, and only ones that exist: the Gold or Platinum card face, saved payees, your name on payment requests, CSV export, and on Platinum several accounts from one phrase. It does not raise a limit, unlock your own money, or buy you priority on any network, because none of that is ours to sell.

What does it cost to use?

The wallet charges nothing. Each payment pays the network fee, which goes to whoever validates the transaction rather than to us. On Base or Polygon that is usually around a cent; on Ethereum it can be considerably more. The network picker sits in the top bar.

Can I try it without risking money?

Yes, and you should before your first real payment. Pick Base Sepolia in the network menu and get free coins from a faucet. It is a real chain, with the same blocks and the same signatures, but its coin is worth nothing, so you can walk the whole path with nothing at stake.

Do I have to verify my identity?

There is no sign-up, no email and no phone. That said, blockchain transactions are public: anyone who knows your address can see its history. It is transparent rather than anonymous, and the two are worth not confusing.

  • Your encrypted wallet. The private key, sealed with your password using scrypt and AES. It is useless to anyone without that password, and it never leaves this browser.
  • Your settings. The network you picked, any custom node address, and your account names.
  • A local copy of your activity. So the list loads instantly. The authoritative record is always the blockchain.
  • Saved payees, on Gold and above. Only names and addresses you chose to keep.

All of it lives in this browser's local storage and is erased the moment you clear site data, or from Settings, under "Erase this wallet from this device". Erasing removes the key too, so keep your twelve words first.
