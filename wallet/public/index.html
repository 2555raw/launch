How it works

Three steps and you're taking payments.

  1. 1

    You make the wallet

    The browser generates twelve words and shows them once. You write them down; we ask for three at random to check you really have them.

  2. 2

    You set a password

    It encrypts the wallet on your device. Without it, what's stored is useless to anyone — including whoever steals your laptop.

  3. 3

    You get paid

    Make a link with the amount and what it's for. Whoever opens it sees the payment filled in and just confirms. The money arrives in seconds.

Security

Why this isn't the same as leaving money on a website.

Most sites that "hold your crypto" are ordinary custody under a new name: your money sits on their server and you hold a promise. Here there is no server that can spend it, because there is no server that knows the key.

The key is born and dies in your browser

Generated with the system's cryptographic source, encrypted with scrypt + AES, and stored only on your device. It never travels over the network, not even encrypted.

No outside script can run here

The signing library is served from this domain, not a CDN, and the content policy blocks every other script. That is how keys get stolen in most cases, and that door is bricked up.

If we disappear, your money doesn't

Your twelve words are an open standard (BIP-39). They work in MetaMask, Rainbow, Ledger or any other wallet. You don't depend on this page staying up.

You can read the code

It's a static page: open your browser's tools and read exactly what it does, line by line. Nothing runs on a server you can't see.

What this is not

What we won't promise you.

  • It is not a debit card. Paying at a shop with a Visa needs a licensed issuer and BIN sponsorship — that's a contract with a bank, not something code can solve. Here, payments are transfers between addresses.
  • It is not a bank, and it is not insured. No deposit protection scheme covers this, because there is no deposit — you are holding your own money.
  • There is no "forgot my password". Nobody can reset it because nobody has it. You recover with your twelve words, and only with those.
  • Payments are final. Chargebacks don't exist. Send to the wrong address and the money is gone. That's why there's a review screen before every signature.
  • The price moves. ETH and POL go up and down. If that keeps you awake, get paid in USDC or USDT.
Questions

What people usually ask.

Can you get at my money?

No, and it isn't a matter of trust — it's architecture. The key that authorises a payment is generated in your browser and stays encrypted on your device. Our server only hands out files; it never receives the key, encrypted or otherwise, so there is nothing for us to spend, freeze, or hand to anyone.

How does topping up from MetaMask work?

Your browser wallet announces itself to the page, you approve a connection, and then you approve one ordinary transfer to your Quiver address. Quiver never sees that wallet's private key — it can only ask it to sign, and you can always say no. Coinbase Wallet, Phantom, Rainbow and the rest work the same way.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Nothing, if you saved your twelve words. Install the wallet on another device, type them in, and the same balance is there. Whoever finds the phone meets an encrypted file and a password they don't know.

And if I lose the twelve words?

Then access to the money is gone for good. This isn't a policy we can bend: no copy of those words exists anywhere except where you put them. Write them on paper and keep that paper the way you'd keep cash.

What does the paid plan actually get me?

Software features, and only ones that exist: the Gold or Platinum card face, saved payees, your name on payment requests, CSV export, and on Platinum several accounts from one phrase. It does not raise a limit, unlock your own money, or buy you priority on any network — none of that is ours to sell.

What does it cost to use?

The wallet charges nothing. Each payment pays the network fee, which goes to whoever validates the transaction, not to us: on Base or Polygon that's usually around a cent; on Ethereum it can be considerably more. The network picker is in the top bar.

Can I try it without risking money?

Yes, and you should before your first real payment. Pick Base Sepolia in the network menu and get free coins from a faucet. It's a real chain, with the same blocks and the same signatures, but its coin is worth nothing — so you can walk the whole path with nothing at stake.

Do I have to verify my identity?

There's no sign-up, no email, no phone. That said, blockchain transactions are public: anyone who knows your address can see its history. It's transparent, not anonymous, and the two are worth not confusing.
